It's something that's been on my mind for some time now: relearning math.

If I was to go back and relearn math again, given what I know now, how would I do it? I think this is an extremely important question, especially since I think I made a few mistakes the first time. 

And these days, everyone is doing crazy stuff in Lean. Not to mention, I have my own perspectives and opinions on how certain aspects of math should be formulated. It makes me wonder if I can relearn mathematics, and in the process, manually formalize the mathematics that I'm aware of. 

In that way, maybe I'll become familiar enough with the Lean language that I can very quickly autoformalize new mathematics.

Besides, next quarter at Stanford, I plan on taking stochastic differential equations and a second course in algebraic geometry. It's almost been a year without me taking a single new class on mathematics, and it's left a major hole in my life satisfaction. It's time I create my own Lean library and graduate to Lean notes.

I think the ideal workflow for my Spring-quarter classes is that I simply take course notes in Lean and have an agent+harness setup for automatically transforming those notes into cleaner LaTeX + natural language docs. That's definitely the bottleneck right now. 

Eventually, I want to enrich my sketches project by attaching Lean certificates to them. I think this is the first step towards that goal.
