When I was a high schooler, I had always wanted to resolve a long-standing unsolved problem in mathematics. One day, to my delight, I found a book on the Riemann-$\zeta$ function and started to read, thinking that one day, I might be a mathematician worthy of finding the proof. In fact, you might recognize the Riemann-$\zeta$ function on the banner of this blog!

Unfortunately I might've gotten a little side-tracked in college after complex analysis. Of course, given another year I probably would've had it (\*wink\*), but it turns out that today two people have beat me to it:
1. Ravi Vakil made an announcement about Alessandro Maria Masullo's human proof of the result: https://math216.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/hurried-announcement-alessandro-masullo-grh/, [currently contested] 
2. Levent Alpöge made an announcement about his proof in collaboration with Claude: https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf. [lean-verified and it appears that Alpöge understands the general argument, ~~but difficult to verify statement equivalence~~]; [UPDATE: https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/]

It's amazing that we live in an age where language models can do mathematics. Some people are even saying that it may be the end of a "romantic era" for mathematics. But for the first time, the limelight is on two comparable proofs of an extremely complex result: one human and one AI assisted (albeit assisting an amazing mathematician).

Perhaps human mathematics isn't "doomed" after all. Plus, even 960 pages is far more readable than hundreds of thousands of pages of formal code. Maybe it's just a matter of time before AI is able to come up with equally concise proofs and without as much human intervention, but somehow it feels far more satisfying and inspiring if a human discovers the solution to Riemann's conjecture independently rather than a swarm of agents in a harness.

If it turns out that one or both these proofs are good, maybe one day I can still propose a different proof and publish it in a fun journal somewhere.... Not to mention, maybe I can meet Alessandro if he's also at Stanford!!
